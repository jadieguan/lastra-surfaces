The Thanksgiving Wines Nobody Else Will Bring.

Eight pairings for the classic dishes that skip the usual Pinot and Riesling, with a bottle under $35 and a splurge for each. Plus three host gifts.

Every November the big wine sites run the same list. Wine Spectator points to Pinot Noir, Beaujolais, Zinfandel, Riesling and Chenin. Decanter leads with Pinot and Chardonnay. They're right. They're also what everyone else is pouring.

This year, try something that makes people ask what's in their glass. Every pick below still follows the rules that make Thanksgiving pairing work: bright acid, low tannin, a little savory edge. They just come from places most tables skip.

One bottle per dish. One you can grab for under $35, one for when you want to make a point. And once the pans are out of the oven and on the landing strip, you'll have a free hand for the corkscrew.

The pairings

01Roast turkey

Etna Rosso

It's the Pinot Noir move, from a volcano. Nerello Mascalese grown on Mount Etna in Sicily is light in color and body, with red cherry, dried herbs and a smoky, ashy edge from the volcanic soil. That savory streak is what makes it work with both the white meat and the dark.

Under $35 Tenuta delle Terre Nere Etna Rosso About $30 to $33 Find it at Chapel Hill Wine Co. ↗
Splurge Tenuta delle Terre Nere "Calderara Sottana" Etna Rosso About $75 A single-vineyard bottling from the same producer. Find it at Italian Wine Merchants ↗
02Sage stuffing

Dry Amontillado sherry

The one that gets the most raised eyebrows, and the one people end up remembering. Dry Amontillado is nutty, salty and savory, like toasted bread and brown butter in a glass. Put it next to stuffing and it tastes like part of the recipe. Pour small glasses, slightly chilled.

Under $35 Lustau "Los Arcos" Dry Amontillado About $15 to $20 Find it at Wine Library ↗
Splurge Valdespino "Tío Diego" Amontillado About $29 A single-vineyard sherry that's more intense and saltier. Find it at Chain Bridge Cellars ↗
03Mashed potatoes and gravy

Aged white Rioja

Not crisp, not buttery. Traditional white Rioja spends years in old barrels and comes out nutty, waxy and a little savory, with enough acid to cut through butter and gravy. It's a white that can stand up to the heaviest thing on the plate.

Under $35 CVNE "Monopole Clásico" Rioja Blanco About $30 Revives an old Rioja style by blending in a little Manzanilla sherry. Find it at Flatiron Wines ↗
Splurge López de Heredia "Viña Gravonia" Crianza Blanco About $90 A cult bottle from one of Rioja's oldest houses. Find it at Lincoln Fine Wines ↗
04Cranberry sauce

Dry Lambrusco di Sorbara

Forget the sweet fizzy Lambrusco from the '80s. Sorbara is the palest, most tart version: pink, dry, sparkling, with raspberry and grapefruit. It matches cranberry's sourness instead of fighting it, and the bubbles reset your palate between bites.

Under $35 Cleto Chiarli "Vecchia Modena" Lambrusco di Sorbara About $20 Find it at Thief Wine ↗
Splurge Paltrinieri "Leclisse" Lambrusco di Sorbara About $28 Lambrusco tops out low, so this is the best of the category, not a big jump in price. Find it at Biondivino ↗
05Sweet potato casserole

Alsace Gewürztraminer

The casserole is sweet and baking-spiced, which flattens most dry wines. Gewürztraminer smells like lychee, rose and ginger, and the Alsace versions carry a touch of richness. Spice meets spice, and the wine doesn't taste thin next to the marshmallows.

Under $35 Famille Hugel Gewurztraminer Classic About $29 Find it at Chapel Hill Wine Co. ↗
Splurge Domaine Zind-Humbrecht Gewurztraminer "Roche Calcaire" About $44 Find it at Bottle Barn ↗
06Roasted squash

Orange wine

Orange wine is white grapes left on their skins, so it drinks with the texture of a red and the brightness of a white. Dried apricot, orange peel and a light grip of tannin line up with caramelized squash, maple and crispy sage. Try it with the maple roasted squash.

Under $35 Field Recordings "Skins" About $25 A California orange wine from Chenin Blanc, Pinot Gris and Albariño. Find it at Hudson Wines ↗
Splurge Radikon Ribolla About $50 for 500ml One of the producers that made orange wine famous. Find it at Coolvines ↗
07Green beans

Santorini Assyrtiko

Green vegetables are hard on wine. Assyrtiko from Santorini is built for it: bone dry, salty, citrusy and sharp. With lemon and almonds on the beans, it reads like a squeeze of lemon on the side.

Under $35 Argyros "Atlantis" Santorini White About $27 Find it at Astor Wines ↗
Splurge Domaine Sigalas Assyrtiko Santorini About $60 Find it at Wine Library ↗
08Pumpkin pie

Madeira

Port is the obvious call. Madeira is the better one. It tastes like caramel, toffee and orange peel, with a sharp acidity that keeps it from being cloying next to the pie. Bonus: an open bottle keeps for months, so leftovers aren't a problem.

Under $35 Blandy's 5 Year Old Malmsey About $28 Find it at Total Wine ↗
Splurge The Rare Wine Co. Historic Series "Boston Bual" About $72 Find it at Astor Wines ↗

About the drips

Eight bottles, one counter

Eight open bottles means drips, bottle rings and at least one tipped glass. On marble or wood, red wine is a race against a stain. On solid stainless, it isn't.

Steel isn't porous, so there's nothing for the wine to soak into and no sealant to wear thin. Let the rings sit until the dishes are done. Then wipe with warm soapy water and dry. That's it. The cleaning guide has the full routine, and it's short.

Host gifts

A good host gift either gets opened that night or saved for later. Each of these has a story to tell when you hand it over.

    Gruet Blanc de Noirs, about $18. Sparkling wine made the Champagne way, in New Mexico, by a family from Champagne. Most people don't know it exists, and it's a crowd-pleaser if the host opens it on the spot. Total Wine ↗
  • 02
    Château Musar Rouge, about $65. Lebanon's most famous red, made in the Bekaa Valley and released only after years of aging. It keeps for decades, so it's a gift the host can save for a birthday or an anniversary. Lincoln Fine Wines ↗
  • 03
    Bollinger Special Cuvée, about $70. Real Champagne from a house that ferments much of it in oak, so it's richer and toastier than most. It's also Wine Spectator rated at 93 points, if the host likes a score. Total Wine ↗
